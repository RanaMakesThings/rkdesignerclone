Scratchy throat followed by five days of intermittent cough with yellow sputum. Symptoms are worse at night. The patient recently developed mild exertional dyspnea, wheeze, and a measured fever of 101.8. Denies hemoptysis or chest pain.

AI clinical primer

The primary focus is distinguishing uncomplicated acute bronchitis from emerging pneumonia or an asthma/COPD-type exacerbation.

Recommended interview paths

1. Assess airway history

The presence of wheeze makes underlying disease highly relevant.

  • History of childhood asthma?
  • Previous inhaler use?
  • Smoking history or pack-years?

2. Gauge functional limitation

“Mild exertional dyspnea” is subjective and needs quantification.

  • Can the patient climb a flight of stairs today?
  • Are they short of breath while talking?

3. Rule out red flags for pneumonia

Fever to 101.8 requires attention. Ask about rigors, chills, pleuritic chest pain on deep inspiration, and whether symptoms are progressively worsening hour by hour.

## 1. What the user is actually trying to get onto the slide

They are **not** asking for a fake product screenshot.

They’re trying to get **one believable clinician-facing artifact** onto the deck that proves, at a glance:

1. the system captured the patient story,
2. turned it into a **clean structured brief** instead of raw transcript sludge,
3. surfaced a **reasoned working set of possibilities**, and
4. made clear **what still needs confirmation** before the clinician decides what to do.

In plain English: the slide needs to make someone say, **“Oh, I get it — the doctor opens this and immediately has the case organized, the open questions exposed, and the next move suggested.”**

That’s the job.

---

## 2. My blunt design opinion on what the artifact should be

Make **one page-like structured brief**.  
Not a dashboard.  
Not a browser screenshot with nav chrome.  
Not a pile of cards pretending to be a product.  
And definitely not a chat transcript.

The hero should feel like a **real pre-visit clinical brief** a clinician could actually scan in 10 seconds.

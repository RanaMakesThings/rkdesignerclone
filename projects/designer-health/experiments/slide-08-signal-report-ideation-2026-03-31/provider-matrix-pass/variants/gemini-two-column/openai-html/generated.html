Considerations to Confirm

Ranked clinical synthesis with concise support, contradictions, and gaps to close.

1.Acute bronchitis

High match
Supports
  • Scratchy throat suggests a preceding upper respiratory irritation.
  • Cough comes in bursts with partial quiet periods.
  • Intermittent yellow sputum can occur without bacterial pneumonia.
Against
  • Nighttime and positional worsening raise alternative causes.
  • Exertional dyspnea would be less typical if prominent.
Unknowns
  • Fever, chills, and overall fatigue burden.
  • Respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, and chest exam.
  • Whether cough is improving or steadily worsening.

2.Community-acquired pneumonia

Moderate match
Supports
  • Persistent productive cough can fit early pneumonia.
  • Yellow or green sputum keeps lower respiratory infection in play.
  • If dyspnea is worsening, probability would rise.
Against
  • No hemoptysis reported.
  • No fever or systemic illness documented so far.
  • No objective findings yet to support consolidation.
Unknowns
  • Temperature, oxygen saturation, and pleuritic chest pain.
  • Focal crackles, asymmetric breath sounds, or imaging findings.

3.GERD-induced cough or post-viral cough

Contrast candidate
Supports
  • Nighttime worsening and lying-flat worsening fit reflux-associated cough.
  • Recent throat irritation could evolve into a lingering post-viral cough.
  • Burst-like coughing can occur with airway hypersensitivity.
Against
  • Productive sputum is less classic for GERD alone.
  • No clear report yet of heartburn, sour taste, or post-meal worsening.
Unknowns
  • Acid reflux symptoms, recent viral illness, and cough duration.
  • Any wheeze, allergic triggers, or prior reactive airway history.

Highest Yield Next Question

Clarifies pneumonia vs bronchitis vs reflux

“Have you had any fever or chills, and are you getting short of breath when walking across a room or climbing stairs?”

This question most efficiently separates routine bronchitic cough from a potentially higher-risk lower respiratory infection, while also framing urgency for vitals, oxygen check, and exam.

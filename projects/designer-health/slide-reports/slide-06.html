Slide 5 — Vox blends adaptive interviewing, clinical reasoning, and medical knowledge to produce a clinician-grade starting point

Slide job

Introduce Vox as the answer and define the success standard.

Core claim

**The value is not in gathering more information. The value is in producing a clinician-grade starting point. Vox is the system built to do that work.**

What the audience should believe after this slide

**Vox is not a form, not a transcript, and not intake automation. It is the product built to turn raw patient story into a verification-ready history clinicians can actually start from.**

Strategic role in deck

This is the hinge between the conceptual half of the deck and the product half. It is now the first full answer slide after Slide 4 establishes both why history is the wedge and why reclaiming it is nontrivial.

It must do three things:

  1. name the answer
  2. show why it is not generic intake or summarization
  3. make the standard feel concrete enough to matter

Current running copy plan

**Header** Vox blends adaptive interviewing, clinical reasoning, and medical knowledge to produce a clinician-grade starting point.

**Subheader** Not a form. Not a transcript. A verification-ready history clinicians can start from.

Current running figure plan

The right direction is no longer a standards placard or adjective card.

The current preferred figure family is:

  • one strong central Vox hero object
  • raw narrative entering from the left
  • adaptive follow-up + reasoning visibly happening inside Vox
  • compact emerging clinician-grade starting point on the right

The key internal labels are minimal and strong:

  • Vox
  • Adaptive clinical interviewer
  • What matters
  • What connects
  • What still needs verification
  • Clinician-grade starting point

Why this is the right answer for Slide 5

Because this slide should feel like the named system built to do the hard work that Slide 4 only lightly introduced.

It should not feel like:

  • a workflow strip
  • an architecture diagram
  • a feature cloud
  • an adjective row

It should feel like:

  • one substantial system
  • visibly doing the heavy lift
  • producing a clinician-grade starting point

Assessment

This is one of the most strategically important slides in the deck. It has improved a lot, but it is still an execution-sensitive slide. The main risk is drift toward either airy marketing language or technical architecture language.

What to avoid

  • workflow-strip look
  • floating capability chips
  • database / brain icon clusters
  • a giant funnel
  • a generic pipeline that replays the old standalone Slide 5
  • a full polished report artifact
  • architecture plumbing language

This is the project-local source of truth for how we actually run the process. The imported manual at inputs/pitch_deck_figure_workflow_manual.md remains the upstream reference, but this file reflects the hardened process after real deck passes in this repo.

Purpose

Use a repeatable loop that keeps strategy, figure decisions, model comparison, and review artifacts organized under one project root.

The goal is not just to make a figure. The goal is to preserve:

  • why the slide exists
  • what the figure is trying to prove
  • what was tried
  • what won
  • how to understand the full pass later without reconstructing context

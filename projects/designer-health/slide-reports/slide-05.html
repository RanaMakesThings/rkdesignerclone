Slide 3 — More efficient visits will increase supply

Slide job

Move from the macro problem to the operating lever.

Core claim

**In a capacity-constrained system, small repeatable gains inside the visit can compound into real clinical capacity.**

What the audience should believe after this slide

**There is a credible throughput lever here, and it is worth asking where the cleanest minutes can be reclaimed.**

Strategic role in deck

This is the bridge slide. It earns permission to care about narrow per-visit efficiency without feeling trivial.

Current running copy plan

**Header** More efficient visits will increase supply.

A small support treatment is acceptable if needed, but the slide should remain sparse.

Current running figure plan

A clean conceptual cumulative visit-efficiency figure.

The right read is:

  • every minute matters
  • small gains compound across schedules
  • this is a throughput argument, not pseudo-precision

What matters conceptually

This slide should not yet identify history. It should not yet explain story-to-structure. It should not yet do financial math.

It just has to make the throughput logic feel credible.

Assessment

This slide is in decent shape. The main guardrail is not to overclaim and not to use fake time anatomy.

What to avoid

  • precise minute splits that are not defensible
  • a pseudo-scientific workflow chart
  • economic math too early
  • introducing the wedge too soon

Slide 4 — History is the cleanest place to reclaim minutes

Slide job

Select the wedge and add the nontriviality bridge. Answer: where specifically inside the visit are the best minutes to reclaim, and why is reclaiming them not simple collection?

Core claim

**History is the cleanest wedge because it is foundational to the encounter, time-variable, and can begin before the encounter — but reclaiming those minutes still requires clinical judgment.**

What the audience should believe after this slide

**History is the right place to focus if the goal is to recover visit time in a meaningful, scalable way — and those minutes are not just clerical collection.**

Strategic role in deck

This is the narrowing slide. It turns the deck from a general throughput story into a specific wedge story.

It is still not yet about Vox. It is still not yet about the final artifact. But it now carries one additional beat that used to belong to standalone Slide 5:

  • this wedge is attractive
  • this wedge is also nontrivial

Current running copy plan

**Header** History is the cleanest place to reclaim minutes.

**Preferred top treatment** No top subheader.

**Bottom ribbon kicker** Why these minutes are harder than they look

**Bottom ribbon line** Clinicians still have to identify the relevant details, organize the timeline, and know what to ask next.

**Bottom ribbon chips**

  • Relevant details
  • Timeline
  • Next questions

Current running figure plan

The correct current direction is the refined single-bar pullout concept with a restrained lower ribbon:

  • one large visit bar at top
  • history segment highlighted
  • clean pull-out / tether into a large blue hero card
  • right side = three clear proof rows/cards
  • bottom = one secondary ribbon introducing the hidden-labor point without becoming a second diagram

The point is:

**here is a normal visit → this is the part we are selecting → here is why this is the wedge → and why reclaiming it is not simple collection**

Why this figure direction works

It preserves what was good in the earlier wedge-selection concept while solving the “thin slide / redundant Slide 5” problem.

It keeps the main logic sparse. It avoids building a full standalone mechanism slide that would compete with Vox. It gives Slide 5 room to become the first full answer slide.

What the right side must prove

The three reasons should feel like argument, not notes:

  • foundational to the encounter
  • time-variable
  • can begin before the encounter

What the bottom ribbon must do

The ribbon should add the nontriviality beat without becoming a second figure.

It should say, in effect:

  • this time is reclaimable
  • this work is not simple

It should not contain:

  • messy patient fragments
  • a mini reasoning workbench
  • a provisional report card
  • anything that turns the slide into a mini product explanation

Assessment

This is a real structural improvement. The slide now carries more weight without requiring a separate old Slide 5. The remaining risk is overstuffing it.

What to avoid

  • keeping a top subheader plus a dense lower ribbon
  • messy narrative fragments here
  • a mini standalone reasoning diagram
  • product-ish language in the hero card
  • anything that competes with Slide 5’s job

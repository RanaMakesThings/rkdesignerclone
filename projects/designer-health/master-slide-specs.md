# Designer / Vox Investor Deck — Master Spec v1.2

## Purpose of this document

This is the current **master operating spec** for the Designer / Vox investor deck.

It is meant to do four things at once:

1. consolidate the current deck story into one coherent source of truth
2. preserve the strategic purpose of each slide, not just the surface copy
3. distinguish what is **locked**, what is **provisionally preferred**, and what is **still live**
4. prevent the deck from drifting backward into older versions of the story

This is not a pure rewrite of prior notes, and it is not a raw dump. It is a **working synthesis** of the current intended deck.

---

# 1. Executive assessment

## Overall read

The deck is now in a meaningfully better place than earlier versions.

The core story is coherent.
The middle of the deck has been substantially clarified.
The product/category distinction is much stronger than before.
The remaining work is now mostly about:

- locking a few slides that still have live visual decisions
- preventing overlap among adjacent slides
- making sure the final deck feels like one argument rather than a sequence of individually decent slides

The deck no longer primarily has a **story problem**.
It primarily has a **version-control and execution problem**.

That is good news.

## Current deck thesis

The strongest current hidden thesis is:

**Vox creates clinical capacity by moving the work of turning patient story into clinician-usable structure upstream, before the visit begins.**

That sentence is the best compression of the whole deck.

It is strong because it contains the whole chain:

- clinical capacity matters
- the mechanism is upstream movement of work
- the work is not documentation, but turning story into usable structure
- the output must be clinician-usable
- the moment of value is before the visit begins

## What is now working well

### 1. The deck has a real center of gravity
Earlier versions risked feeling like a loose healthcare AI deck.
The current version is much sharper.

The strongest version of the story is not:

- AI helps doctors with paperwork
- AI makes intake better
- AI helps around the visit

The strongest version is:

- **AI can change the starting conditions of the visit by producing clinician-usable structure before reasoning begins**

That is a more valuable and more defensible story.

### 2. The mid-deck separation is now better controlled
This is still the single most important structural improvement.

The key middle-slide distinction now reads:

- **Slide 4 = why history, and why reclaiming it is not trivial**
- **Slide 5 = what is Vox, and what bar must the output meet?**
- **Slide 6 = how the workflow operates**
- **Slide 7 = what the clinician actually starts from**

Removing the standalone old Slide 5 is helpful because it reduces the risk that the middle of the deck turns into three slightly different versions of the same story-to-structure diagram.

### 3. The category story is better
Earlier versions of the deck were at higher risk of collapse into:

- intake
- summary generation
- pre-charting
- scribe extension
- generic assistive AI

The newer category-defense thinking makes the point much better:

- clinician buy-in for AI is already real
- most competitive density has formed in documentation
- the lane for tools built to change the visit itself is still much thinner

That is strategically stronger than a timing-only claim or a vague “we are different” slide.

### 4. The deck has a real close
Slides 9–12 now hang together as a clean final arc:

- economics
- proof path
- team fit
- milestone-based raise

That is much better than a generic end-of-deck fadeout.

## What is still vulnerable

### 1. The deck can still collapse into weaker adjacent categories if Slides 4, 5, 7, or 8 are weak
These are still the most strategically sensitive slides.

If merged Slide 4 feels like simple collection, or Slide 5 looks like airy feature-marketing, or Slide 7 looks like a generic summary artifact, or Slide 8 becomes a weak comparison slide, the whole deck softens.

### 2. The middle slides can still become too visually similar
This is still the main execution risk.

If Slide 4, Slide 5, and Slide 6 all end up being some variation of:

- selected input
- system area
- output object

then the conceptual separation will be lost even if the copy is good.

### 3. Slide 8 is strategically right but still design-sensitive
This slide has become clearer conceptually, but it still needs careful execution.

It must not become:

- a competitor matrix
- an anti-scribe attack slide
- a workflow explainer
- a semantic claim about difference without a visual argument
- a timing argument about when software runs rather than where its value lands

### 4. The late slides still depend on specifics
Slides 11 and 12 are structurally clear but still need final inputs:

- founder-specific trust lines
- final raise / milestone specifics

**Do not let this become a documentation story.**

## Slide 2 — Demand is outpacing clinical capacity

### Slide job

Establish that the problem is real, structural, worsening, and already visible to patients.

### Core claim

**There are not enough clinical slots relative to demand, and the strain is already showing up in access.**

### What the audience should believe after this slide

**There is a real capacity/access problem here, and it is severe enough that improving throughput would matter.**

### Strategic role in deck

This is the first true argument slide.
It chooses the kind of problem the company is solving.

It is not saying:

- burnout is bad
- paperwork is annoying
- healthcare is inefficient
- intake is messy

It is saying:

- **there is a clinical-capacity problem with real access consequences**

### Current running copy plan

**Header**
Demand is outpacing clinical capacity.

No subheader is required unless the final visual wants a tiny support line.

### Current running figure plan

The strongest preferred direction remains:

**One month to the next available appointment**

Meaning:

- spatialize the 31-day wait as absence of available slots
- make “31 days” feel literal and painful
- optionally pair with one patient-consequence tile

### Why this figure direction is best

Because it makes capacity constraint feel like the **absence of openings**, which is exactly what the rest of the deck will later try to address.

It is more visceral than a normal chart.
It is more memorable than two stat tiles.
It still feels premium if done carefully.

### Secondary figure fallback

If needed, use:

- benchmark trend + patient consequence

But that is more conventional and less memorable.

### Assessment

Conceptually strong.
This slide is not the current weak point.
The main work is execution quality and ensuring the visual feels elegant rather than gimmicky.

### What to avoid

- too many unrelated stats
- a policy report feeling
- a generic line chart unless beautifully done
- geographic maps
- healthcare macro wallpaper

### Current status

**Conceptually locked; execution still matters.**

## Slide 4 — History is the cleanest place to reclaim minutes

### Slide job

Select the wedge and add the nontriviality bridge.
Answer: where specifically inside the visit are the best minutes to reclaim, and why is reclaiming them not simple collection?

### Core claim

**History is the cleanest wedge because it is foundational to the encounter, time-variable, and can begin before the encounter — but reclaiming those minutes still requires clinical judgment.**

### What the audience should believe after this slide

**History is the right place to focus if the goal is to recover visit time in a meaningful, scalable way — and those minutes are not just clerical collection.**

### Strategic role in deck

This is the narrowing slide.
It turns the deck from a general throughput story into a specific wedge story.

It is still not yet about Vox.
It is still not yet about the final artifact.
But it now carries one additional beat that used to belong to standalone Slide 5:

- this wedge is attractive
- this wedge is also nontrivial

### Current running copy plan

**Header**
History is the cleanest place to reclaim minutes.

**Preferred top treatment**
No top subheader.

**Bottom ribbon kicker**
Why these minutes are harder than they look

**Bottom ribbon line**
Clinicians still have to identify the relevant details, organize the timeline, and know what to ask next.

**Bottom ribbon chips**
- Relevant details
- Timeline
- Next questions

### Current running figure plan

The correct current direction is the refined single-bar pullout concept with a restrained lower ribbon:

- one large visit bar at top
- history segment highlighted
- clean pull-out / tether into a large blue hero card
- right side = three clear proof rows/cards
- bottom = one secondary ribbon introducing the hidden-labor point without becoming a second diagram

The point is:

**here is a normal visit → this is the part we are selecting → here is why this is the wedge → and why reclaiming it is not simple collection**

### Why this figure direction works

It preserves what was good in the earlier wedge-selection concept while solving the “thin slide / redundant Slide 5” problem.

It keeps the main logic sparse.
It avoids building a full standalone mechanism slide that would compete with Vox.
It gives Slide 5 room to become the first full answer slide.

### What the right side must prove

The three reasons should feel like argument, not notes:

- foundational to the encounter
- time-variable
- can begin before the encounter

### What the bottom ribbon must do

The ribbon should add the nontriviality beat without becoming a second figure.

It should say, in effect:

- this time is reclaimable
- this work is not simple

It should not contain:

- messy patient fragments
- a mini reasoning workbench
- a provisional report card
- anything that turns the slide into a mini product explanation

### Assessment

This is a real structural improvement.
The slide now carries more weight without requiring a separate old Slide 5.
The remaining risk is overstuffing it.

### What to avoid

- keeping a top subheader plus a dense lower ribbon
- messy narrative fragments here
- a mini standalone reasoning diagram
- product-ish language in the hero card
- anything that competes with Slide 5’s job

### Current status

**Concept locked; execution still live.**

2. the competitive landscape is much thinner for tools that change the visit itself than for tools focused on documentation

**Vox is built to change the visit itself, not just the record it produces.**

**Clinician buy-in for AI is already real. But most competition is concentrated in documentation, while the landscape for tools that change the visit itself is still much thinner.**

Direct-surface mode.

The locked audience-belief line is strong enough to serve as the header.
The locked core-claim line is strong enough to serve as the subheader.
Do not translate these into a new header/subheader pair unless layout later forces compression.

### Strategic role in deck

This is the category-defense slide.
It is not mainly about saying “we are unique.”
It is about preventing the audience from slotting Vox into the wrong category.

The slide is now about:

- validated clinician AI adoption
- dense competition in documentation
- a thinner lane for tools that change the visit itself

**Header**
Vox is built to change the visit itself, not just the record it produces.

**Subheader**
Clinician buy-in for AI is already real. But most competition is concentrated in documentation, while the landscape for tools that change the visit itself is still much thinner.

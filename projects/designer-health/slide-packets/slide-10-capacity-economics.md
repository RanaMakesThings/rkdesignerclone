# Slide 10 — Capacity Economics

## Status

- current deck numbering: slide 10
- decision state: active packet
- role in deck sequence:
  follows slide `9` and precedes slide `11`

## Why this slide exists

- convert the operational story into economic consequence
- show that reclaimed time matters only when it becomes completed visits
- make the value legible without turning the slide into spreadsheet analysis
- bridge from category credibility to go-to-market credibility

## Locked slide thesis

Recovered visit time creates real capacity when recovered minutes become
completed visits.

## Zoomed-out deck role

- slide `9` says the visit still starts from zero
- slide `10` says fixing that gap has real economic consequence
- slide `11` then says where to start proving it in the market

So this slide is the investor bridge from product/category logic to practical
business leverage.

## Figure job

- make one base-case economics story feel immediate and credible
- show range without looking like a finance worksheet
- anchor on `+1 visit / provider / day`
- make the annual and monthly implications easy to scan
- keep the assumptions visible but compressed and quiet

## Locked copy architecture

- header:
  `Recovered visit time creates real capacity.`
- subheader:
  `When recovered minutes become completed visits, the impact is meaningful.`
- hero anchors:
  - `+1 visit / provider / day`
  - `$11,000 / year`
  - `~$917 / month break-even spend / provider`
- assumptions:
  - `220 clinic days`
  - `$125 / visit`
  - `40% contribution margin`
- scenario 1:
  `Conservative — $7,920 / year — +1 / day at $90 / visit`
- scenario 2:
  `Base — $11,000 / year — +1 / day at $125 / visit`
- scenario 3:
  `Upside — $22,000 / year — +2 / day at $125 / visit`
- bottom line:
  `Recovered time matters when it becomes completed visits.`

## Current best direction

- one dominant hero economics block
- three smaller scenario tiles as range checks
- one quiet assumption line
- one interpretive bottom line

This is probably the right concept family.
The main open question is execution: how to make it feel like operating leverage
instead of a sterile KPI card set.

## Current read

- strategically, the current idea is correct
- the slide should not explain the math at length
- the hero block should do the persuasive work
- the scenario tiles should validate range, not compete for attention
- if the slide starts reading like a table or finance memo, it has failed

## Visual rules

- premium investor-deck feel, not spreadsheet analysis
- hero scenario must dominate
- scenario tiles should be easy to compare at a glance
- assumptions should be quiet and compressed
- avoid large tables, dense footnotes, or calculator-looking treatment
- avoid looking too generic or too dashboard-like

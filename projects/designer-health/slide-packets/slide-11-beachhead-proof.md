# Slide 11 — Beachhead Proof

## Status

- current deck numbering: slide 11
- decision state: active packet
- role in deck sequence:
  follows slide `10` and precedes slide `12`

## Why this slide exists

- turn the economics claim into a concrete go-to-market choice
- show that the first market is chosen for speed of proof, not abstract size
- define the beachhead by operational shape rather than by a single specialty
- make the beachhead feel product-specific rather than consulting-generic
- define what the first pilot must prove so the market choice feels testable

## Locked slide thesis

The right beachhead is any care setting where Vox can prove real clinical
throughput quickly because visit pressure is visible, buying is simple, and
deployment friction is low.

## Zoomed-out deck role

- slide `10` says throughput gains matter economically
- slide `11` says where to prove those gains first in the market
- slide `12` then asks whether the team is credible enough to execute that plan

So this slide is the bridge from economics to go-to-market discipline.

## Slide purpose brief

- top claim:
  the best first market is not one specialty but a repeatable operating profile:
  settings where throughput pain is visible, buying is concentrated, and proof
  shows up quickly in live clinical operations
- support claim:
  the choice should feel like a practical deployment decision, not a category
  or market-size abstraction
- non-goals:
  - do not turn this into a TAM, SAM, SOM, or sizing slide
  - do not use filter, gate, or funnel clichés unless every simpler composition
    fails
  - do not make the rationale so generic that it could describe any healthcare
    workflow startup

## Figure job

- make one beachhead profile explicit: `High-throughput groups with simple deployment`
- keep example settings visible but clearly de-emphasized
- attach three reasons that feel specific to Vox deployment and proof
- make the pilot scorecard concrete enough that investors can imagine the first
  60 to 90 days
- keep the full composition easy to scan in one sweep

## Locked copy architecture

- header:
  `Start where throughput proof is fastest.`
- subheader:
  `The best first market is any setting with visible visit pressure, simple`
  `buying, and low deployment friction.`
- hero beachhead label:
  `High-throughput groups with simple deployment`
- hero support line:
  `A profile, not a specialty`
- example-setting labels:
  - `Primary care`
  - `GI / gastroenterology`
  - `Orthopedics`
  - `Urgent care`
  - `Multi-site specialty groups`
- proof reasons:
  - `throughput pain is visible in every clinic day`
  - `buying and rollout are concentrated`
  - `pilot outcomes are measurable fast`
- proof panel title:
  `What the first pilot must prove`
- proof panel rows:
  - `patient completion before visit`
  - `clinician verification time`
  - `re-asking reduction in the room`
  - `visit throughput or schedule lift`
- optional bottom line:
  `Pick the profile where proof happens fastest.`

## Current best direction

- one dominant highlighted beachhead profile card
- one row or cluster of quiet example-setting cards
- three attached reason tags, rails, or side labels
- one compact proof scorecard nearby

This is the lead direction.
It keeps the market choice explicit without pretending the wedge is locked to a
single specialty.

## Figure directions to explore

- direction 1:
  beachhead profile hero plus pilot proof scorecard
  - one blue hero profile card
  - faint example-setting cards orbiting or lining up around it
  - three attached reasons around the hero card
  - separate proof scorecard on the right
  - best for clarity and investor readability
- direction 2:
  shape-not-specialty field
  - one dominant profile card in the center
  - faint surrounding settings shown as examples, not rejected alternatives
  - proof scorecard integrated beneath or beside the hero card
  - best if we want the slide to feel more premium and make the cross-specialty
    point explicit
- direction 3:
  qualifying-conditions strip
  - horizontal strip anchored by the three qualifying conditions
  - example settings appear only after or beneath the conditions
  - proof metrics panel on the right
  - only worth testing if it stays simpler than a funnel

## Current read

- strategically, the slide should be about proof speed, not just market fit
- the slide should define a repeatable first-market profile, not name one
  specialty as the whole wedge
- the three reasons should stay grounded in deployment and measurement, not
  vague market attractiveness
- the proof panel should feel like a real pilot readout, not a generic KPI list
- if the slide starts to resemble a consulting framework, it has failed

## Visual rules

- white or warm-light background
- grayscale candidate settings
- one restrained blue accent on the chosen beachhead
- premium deck feel, not sales-ops or consulting template
- strong hierarchy: beachhead first, reasons second, proof panel third
- avoid crowded matrix layouts, scoring grids, or verbose explanation blocks
- avoid layouts that imply only one specialty qualifies

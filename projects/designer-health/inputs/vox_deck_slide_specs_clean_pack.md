# VOX Deck Slide Specs — Clean Pack

## Purpose

This document is the clean companion to the figure workflow manual.

It is intentionally limited to **slide-by-slide strategic specs**.
It does **not** include:
- render queues
- prototype sequencing
- critique logs
- revision history
- execution management notes
- HTML / SVG / image-generation prompts

Use this document when you want the model to know **what each slide should be**, without biasing it with prior render logistics or iteration history.

---

## Deck narrative spine

The deck moves through this logic:

1. There is a supply-demand mismatch.
2. One way to create supply is to recover visit minutes.
3. History is the most moveable part of those minutes.
4. Those minutes are consumed turning story into structure.
5. That only matters if the output is clinician-grade.
6. Vox is built to produce that kind of history.
7. If it works, the category is ready, the economics are real, and the first market is clear.

---

## Visual sibling groups

### Slides 3–5
These are the conceptual core.
They should feel like one visual family and one argument.

- Slide 3 = where time is lost
- Slide 4 = what that time is spent doing
- Slide 5 = why that section is the best intervention point

### Slides 6–8
These are the product / proof-of-work family.
They should become slightly more concrete and more card / artifact oriented.

### Slides 9–11
These are the category / economics / GTM family.
They should feel sharper, more evaluative, and more strategic.

### Slides 12–13
These should simplify rather than intensify.

---

## Cross-slide design rules

- White background
- Grayscale base
- One restrained blue accent
- Rounded geometry
- Generous whitespace
- One clear hero object per slide
- Thin callout / bracket logic where helpful
- No fake software chrome unless the artifact itself is the point
- No decorative infographic clutter

- one thin horizontal rule below the subheader

### Object inventory
- bird mark
- VOX wordmark
- subheader

### Slide takeaway
Small per-visit gains compound into real capacity.

### Figure job

### Selected direction
Segmented hero visit bar plus quieter repeated mini-bars.

### Core idea
One highlighted visit segment shows where time is most moveable; repeated small gains across visits visibly create one additional visit.

### Composition
- top: one long segmented visit bar with faint minute ruler
- bracket / callout attached to history segment
- bottom: quieter repeated visit bars showing compounding into one new visit

### Text inventory
- Header: More efficient visits will increase supply
- Chips: Every minute matters / Small per-visit gains compound
- Top bar labels: History reconstruction / Decisions + counseling / Exam + workflow
- Bracket themes: most variable minutes / best target for upstream prep / part AI can help most

### Object inventory
- minute ruler
- 1 long segmented hero bar
- 1 bracket callout
- repeated mini-bars

### Emphasis rules

Create a premium conceptual slide showing a typical visit as one segmented bar with the history portion highlighted and called out as the most variable, most moveable part of the visit. Beneath it, show repeated modest efficiency across many visits gradually creating enough capacity for one additional visit. Keep the figure conceptual, not forensic.

## Slide 4 — Visits start with story, not structure

### Purpose
Create the aha mechanism.

### Slide takeaway
Valuable visit time is spent turning patient story into decision-ready structure.

### Figure job
Show story on the left, usable structure on the right, and make the middle feel like scarce cognitive work.

### Selected direction
Narrative bridge / membrane.

### Core idea
One human story enters on the left. A soft broad bridge in the middle shows the real translation work. A quiet structured card on the right shows the usable result. The middle communicates through grouping and flow, not prose.

### Composition
- centered horizontal hero figure
- left narrative panel
- broad rounded transformation bridge in the middle
- right structured card
- thin callout beneath the bridge

### Text inventory
- Left label: Patient story
- Left paragraph: This started maybe three days ago. It’s worse when I’m walking around. I was up a lot last night because of it. I don’t think I’ve had a fever. I’ve had something kind of like this before.
- Middle label: Turn story into structure
- Callout: Essential work that can start before the visit
- Right label: Decision-ready structure
- Right sections:
  - Relevant positives / negatives
    - Worse with walking
    - No fever
  - Timeline
    - 3-day course
    - Worse overnight
  - What to verify
    - Prior similar episode?
    - Trigger / severity?

### Object inventory
- soft narrative panel
- paragraph block
- 3 subtle highlight sweeps in the paragraph
- broad rounded bridge / membrane
- 3 faint internal horizontal bands
- thin callout
- structured card
- 3 section headers
- 6 short rows

### Emphasis rules
- middle bridge is the hero
- accent color lives mainly in the bridge
- left panel is softer and lower contrast
- right card is clean but secondary
- the middle should not need to be read

### Avoid
- tables in the middle
- sentence-to-output rows
- workflow boxes
- bullets on the left
- fake UI on the right
- thick arrows
- funnels

### Concise prose spec
Create a premium horizontal conceptual figure on white. On the left, place a soft rounded panel labeled “Patient story,” containing one compact paragraph of natural language. Within the paragraph, add three very subtle highlight sweeps to hint at latent idea clusters. In the center, place a broad rounded directional bridge labeled “Turn story into structure.” The bridge should read like an organizing membrane, not a table, and should contain only three faint internal bands with no readable text. Beneath it, attach a thin callout reading “Essential work that can start before the visit.” On the right, place a clean card labeled “Decision-ready structure,” containing three sections with short content rows.

### Object inventory

Create a conceptual slide that feels like a zoom-in on the highlighted history segment from slide 3. Use one large rounded accent band labeled “History reconstruction” as the hero object, then attach three clean secondary callouts explaining that this work is retold at every visit, time-variable, and able to begin before the encounter. Keep the whole slide in the same restrained family as slide 3.

## Slide 9 — The visit still starts from zero

### Purpose
Define the category gap.

### Slide takeaway
Assistive tools exist, but the start-of-visit gap remains open.

### Figure job
Locate the unmet need spatially in the care journey.

### Selected direction
Workflow-gap journey strip.

### Core idea
Map the care journey from before the visit through after the visit. Show adjacent parts of the workflow as increasingly assisted, but leave the “start of visit” zone visibly open and highlighted as the unresolved problem.

### Composition
- one horizontal journey strip across the slide
- three zones: Before visit / Start of visit / During & after
- supported zones lightly filled
- open zone outlined or accent-highlighted

### Text inventory
- Header: The visit still starts from zero
- Subheader: Clinicians have shown they will adopt assistive tools. The open problem is starting the visit with better structure.
- Zone labels:
  - Before visit
  - Start of visit
  - During / after
- Supported-task tags:
  - reminders
  - scheduling
  - documentation support
  - note generation
  - clerical relief
- Open-zone ideas:
  - structured starting point
  - pre-decision signal
  - recovered decision time

### Object inventory
- 1 journey strip
- 3 zones
- a small number of supported-task tags
- 1 highlighted open zone

### Emphasis rules
- the open zone is the hero
- supported zones remain quiet and credible
- this should not read as an anti-scribe slide

### Avoid
- competitor matrices
- two-column comparisons
- heavy commentary
- too many tags

### Concise prose spec
Create a clean workflow-gap map across the care journey. Divide the strip into Before visit, Start of visit, and During / after. Show adjacent tasks as increasingly supported with light neutral fills and small tags. Leave the Start of visit zone visibly open and accent-highlighted to show that clinicians still begin the encounter without enough structure.

### Text inventory

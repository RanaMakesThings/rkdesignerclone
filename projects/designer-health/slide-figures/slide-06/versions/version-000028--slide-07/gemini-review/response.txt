**1. Verdict**
This figure is working well. Removing the floating handoff brief was the right call; the slide now feels like a calm, premium, and restrained workflow diagram rather than a cluttered software schematic. The linear progression is obvious at a glance, and the "Structure" step rightfully commands attention. 

**2. What works**
*   The light green fill on the "Structure" card perfectly establishes it as the visual star without breaking the clean, flat aesthetic.
*   The nested pill design (e.g., "Secure link", "Right patient") successfully conveys a sense of structured data without resorting to cheesy fake UI.
*   The four-step strip is clean, evenly spaced, and highly scannable from left to right.

**3. What is still broken**
*   The connective dotted lines between the cards are far too faint; they almost disappear, making the cards look like isolated islands rather than a continuous flow.
*   The floating pills at the bottom of the Structure card ("PROVENANCE", "EXPLICIT UNKNOWNS") and Verify card ("VERIFICATION QUEUE") look like disconnected afterthoughts that break the tidy internal hierarchy of the cards.
*   The text contrast inside the light grey/green pills is slightly too low, making it harder to read from a distance.
*   The tiny circles on the connective lines add unnecessary visual noise; a simple, slightly thicker arrow would be cleaner.

**4. Is the handoff now too implicit?**
No. The text does the heavy lifting perfectly: Step 3 ends with "Brief prepared for handoff," and Step 4 begins with "Review handoff." You do not need a separate bridge object cluttering the space between them. The logical relationship is clear, even if the visual connection (the faint line) needs a slight boost.

**5. Best next move**
Darken and slightly thicken the connective lines and arrows between the four main cards so the visual momentum actually carries the viewer from left to right. Additionally, either integrate the floating bottom pills ("Provenance", "Explicit Unknowns", "Verification Queue") into the main nested boxes above them, or delete them entirely to maintain the strict, clean boundaries of the cards.

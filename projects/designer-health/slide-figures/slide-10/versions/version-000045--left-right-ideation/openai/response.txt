## 2. What should the left side show?

## Best answer:
**Show just a couple shortened bars plus a green `New visit`.**

Specifically:
- **2 shortened visit bars**
- a subtle cue that reclaimed time accumulates
- **1 green `New visit` block** at the end

This is better than the alternatives:

### Better than a before/after mini-comparison
A before/after introduces more interpretation work:
- “before what?”
- “after what?”
- “how many visits am I comparing?”
It risks becoming explanatory rather than referential.

### Better than a single unlocked slot icon/object alone
A single slot alone may be too abstract. You lose the explicit callback to slide 3’s visit bars, which is the whole point of the left side.

### So the sweet spot is:
**not a comparison, not a standalone icon — a tiny continuation of slide 3’s motif.**

Think:
`shortened visit` + `shortened visit` → `New visit`

That’s enough.

## 3. Should the right side include a secondary `+2` upside check?

## Recommendation:
**Keep the main right-side proof focused on `+1 visit / provider / day → $11,000 / year`.**

If you include `+2`, make it **very quiet** and clearly secondary.

Why:
- The slide’s job is to make **one added visit feel real and monetizable**.
- A visible `+2` scenario can weaken credibility by making the base case feel like a setup for upside selling.
- Investors usually trust a **tight conservative proof** more than a slide trying to show range on the same plane.

### Best practice:
Include `+2` only as:
- a tiny muted note below the assumptions, or
- a very faint “Upside: ~2x impact at +2 visits/day” footnote-style line

Do **not** give it equal visual weight, separate math, or another big number.

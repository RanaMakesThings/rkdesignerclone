## Diagnosis

- the prior round was conceptually right but compositionally too even
- the slide lost force because the panels behaved like balanced modules
- the next round should preserve the family but harden the hierarchy

## Core composition

- header stays exactly as-is
- lower half becomes a two-panel comparison with controlled asymmetry
- the right panel should be slightly wider or otherwise feel slightly more
  dominant than the left
- the cards still belong to the same family, but they should not read as twins

## Panel anatomy

- kicker
- large image window
- shorter, stronger support line
- tiny density label
- chip field

## Hierarchy rules

- image windows should be taller and visually anchor each card
- support lines should use stronger size, weight, or contrast than a caption
- density labels should be tiny but explicit:
  - `More crowded`
  - `Still thin`
- left chip field should feel tight and shelf-like
- right chip field should feel sparse on purpose

## Direction A: Strategic Asymmetry

- visual protagonist:
  right card slightly larger, with more authority
- density signal:
  left proves adoption; right signals under-served territory
- why it works:
  it matches the slide thesis directly
- main risk:
  if pushed too far, the pair stops feeling related

## Direction B: Image-Led Territory

- visual protagonist:
  both cards become more image-dominant, with the copy compressed into sharper
  supporting arguments
- density signal:
  the chip fields read like market conditions under the scenes
- why it works:
  it removes the light-UI-card feeling from the prior round
- main risk:
  the support lines can become too small again if not enforced

## Cross-direction rules

- no crop, clip, or zoom of the locked graphics
- no extra explanatory text beyond the defined header, kickers, support lines,
  density labels, and chips
- the right field must feel intentionally thin, not empty by accident
- the left field can feel crowded, but it must stay controlled
- preserve the deck's premium editorial restraint

## Figure Job

- locate the unmet need spatially in the care journey
- show adjacent workflow support as real context
- make `Start of visit` the unresolved hero zone

## Direction 1: Clean Rounded Journey Strip

- visual protagonist:
  - one rounded horizontal strip with three equal zones
- secondary elements:
  - a few support pills in the outer zones
  - sparse unmet-need cues in the middle
- figure family:
  - `care_journey_gap_map`
- why it stays in the set:
  - clearest native interpretation
  - fastest to read
  - lowest taste risk
- main risk:
  - may feel slightly abstract if the middle gap is too empty

## Direction 2: Anchored Timeline with Hollow Node

- visual protagonist:
  - one timeline bar with three major nodes and a hollow center node
- secondary elements:
  - small support tags on the outer nodes
  - tiny unmet-need labels beneath the hollow center
- figure family:
  - `care_journey_gap_timeline`
- why it stays in the set:
  - more compressed and iconic than the strip
  - could make the unresolved center feel sharper
- main risk:
  - can become too symbolic and lose the workflow texture

## Direction 3: Clinical Hallway Overlaid

- visual protagonist:
  - quiet clinic hallway photo with a semi-transparent journey strip overlay
- secondary elements:
  - small support tags in the outer zones
  - center cutout or outlined window over the hallway
- figure family:
  - `care_journey_gap_photo_overlay`
- why it stays in the set:
  - strongest photo branch
  - gives real clinical context without showing people
- main risk:
  - depends heavily on image calmness and overlay legibility

## Direction 4: Hands and Clipboard

- visual protagonist:
  - clinician hands holding a clipboard with the journey overlay aligned to the
    page surface
- secondary elements:
  - quiet pills in the outer zones
  - outlined center window with unmet-need cues
- figure family:
  - `care_journey_gap_hybrid_clipboard`
- why it stays in the set:
  - strongest hybrid branch
  - makes the missing starting structure feel tangible
- main risk:
  - can look like product/UI overlay if the card geometry gets too literal

## Cross-option design rules

- keep the deck grammar: white or very light base, grayscale structure, one
  restrained blue accent
- `Start of visit` must dominate first
- supported zones must stay credible but quiet
- this cannot read as a competitor matrix or anti-scribe slide
- use only a small number of support tags

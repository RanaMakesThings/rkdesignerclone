## Figure job

  visit

## Direction 1: Balanced Editorial Diptych

- visual protagonist:
  - two equal soft cards with equal image windows
- copy placement:
  - kicker above image
  - support line below image
  - chip treatment below support line
- density signal:
  - left gets a tight 2x2 chip cluster
  - right gets one blue `VOX` chip and open field
- why it works:
  - it is the clearest expression of the brief
  - it keeps the slide calm and deck-native
- main risk:
  - if the cards become too symmetrical, the strategic thinness on the right
    can flatten out

## Direction 2: Open-Right Contrast

- visual protagonist:
  - same card pair, but the right card has noticeably more internal breathing
    room
- copy placement:
  - same vertical stack as Direction 1
- density signal:
  - left cluster sits tighter and reads almost like a market shelf
  - right chip sits lower with more surrounding empty space
- why it works:
  - it sharpens the market-density argument without adding more text
- main risk:
  - if pushed too far, the right card can start to feel unfinished instead of
    strategically open

## Direction 3: Quiet Gallery Mount

- visual protagonist:
  - the cards feel slightly more editorial, like mounted plates on a white wall
- copy placement:
  - kicker and support line live inside each card block but with thinner chrome
- density signal:
  - chip areas feel anchored to a shared low baseline
- why it stays in the set:
  - this is the most premium / restrained variant
- main risk:
  - it can become too tasteful and under-signal the left/right density contrast

## Cross-direction rules

- no center object
- no workflow arrows
- no extra prose beyond the header and the two panel lines
- same image size on both sides
- full image visible on both sides with no crop or clipping
- the right card must carry more whitespace than the left
- the right chip field should never look crowded

## Recommended lead

- direction:
  `Balanced Editorial Diptych`
- why:
  it obeys the brief most directly and leaves enough room for subtle right-side
  openness without making the slide feel under-designed

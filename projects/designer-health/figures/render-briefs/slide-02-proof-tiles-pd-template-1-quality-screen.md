## Screen

### Booked-Out Month View

- verdict:
  - `keep with upgrade`
- why:
  - the underlying idea is strong because a month-view calendar instantly maps
    to the `~1 month` claim
  - the problem is not the concept; it is that the object gets busy quickly and
    can start looking like a weak software screenshot
- what would make it good:
  - crop to only four visible weeks
  - suppress most date detail
  - show fullness through disciplined repeated booking marks, not text labels
  - make the week-4 opening unmistakable
- creative push:
  - make the open date feel like a single quiet point of relief in an otherwise
    saturated field
- why it could still fail:
  - if we over-detail it, it collapses into clutter immediately

### Schedule Columns

- verdict:
  - `keep`
- why:
  - this is the strongest direct expression of constrained capacity
  - it reads systemically, fits the deck tone, and does not rely on a lucky
    stock image
- what would make it good:
  - sparse provider labels
  - elegant slot rhythm
  - one isolated late opening
  - metrics kept fully outside the schedule object
- creative push:
  - make the strip feel like a real clinic schedule artifact rather than a grid
    by varying slot density and leaving a few small gaps
- why it could still fail:
  - if it starts to look like a dashboard, it loses the deck feel

### Next Available Slot

- verdict:
  - `keep`
- why:
  - very legible and very deck-friendly
  - it makes the wait visible without needing a full calendar
- what would make it good:
  - the week cards have to feel like appointment artifacts, not blank boxes
  - the week-4 opening has to feel real, with one actual time
- creative push:
  - make the weekly progression feel like a quiet drumbeat of delay rather than
    a literal timeline
- why it could still fail:
  - if the cards are too generic, it turns back into meaningless rectangles

### Search to Slot to Care Path

- verdict:
  - `keep with upgrade`
- why:
  - the logic is strong because it unifies both proof stats into one causal
    system
  - but it is very easy for this to become over-explained or consulting-y
- what would make it good:
  - use almost no words on the path itself
  - rely on two concrete endpoint objects rather than labeled process bubbles
  - let the branch do the work
- creative push:
  - make the middle stage feel like an anxious search through scarce
    availability without literally drawing a search UI
- why it could still fail:
  - too much explanatory copy would flatten it immediately

### Demand Queue Visualization

- verdict:
  - `keep with upgrade`
- why:
  - the supply-demand logic is excellent
  - the current risk is that it can become metaphorical instead of clinical
- what would make it good:
  - use appointment request cards, not circles or abstract queue marks
  - make the bottleneck look like limited appointment capacity, not a sales
    funnel
  - keep the dropped request subtle
- creative push:
  - make the queue feel compressed and pressurized without resorting to arrows
    or cartoon tension
- why it could still fail:
  - if the objects lose their appointment-specific identity, it becomes generic

### Appointment Card Sequence

- verdict:
  - `keep`
- why:
  - the artifact logic is strong and more tactile than the other native options
  - it can feel premium if the cards are well-designed
- what would make it good:
  - distinct date progression
  - minimal but believable microcontent
  - one unavailable card kept subordinate
- creative push:
  - make the cards feel almost physical, like appointment slips passing through
    time, without becoming skeuomorphic
- why it could still fail:
  - repeated cloned cards would make it look fake fast

### Reception Window Photo

- verdict:
  - `keep with upgrade`
- why:
  - stock can help here because the scheduling problem is real-world and
    operational
  - but this only works if the photo is quiet enough to support the stats
- what would make it good:
  - a desk-forward crop with hands, notebook, phone, or scheduling surface
  - minimal face emphasis
  - flattened grayscale treatment
- creative push:
  - use the photo as context only, almost like a documentary still, with the
    metrics feeling analytical beneath it
- why it could still fail:
  - generic stock energy would weaken the whole slide immediately

### Appointment Book Photo Card

- verdict:
  - `discard`
- why:
  - the concept is too dependent on finding a stock image that looks both real
    and specifically scheduling-relevant
  - most candidate images drift toward vague stationery or office props
- what would make it good:
  - a genuinely strong scheduling artifact photo, which we do not currently
    have
- creative push:
  - none worth chasing now
- why it could still fail:
  - even a decent image would still likely feel arbitrary next to the stronger
    native directions

### Phone and Calendar Diptych

- verdict:
  - `keep with upgrade`
- why:
  - this is the strongest high-upside photo/hybrid concept because it can show
    two sides of the same access problem
  - it has more conceptual richness than a single stock photo
- what would make it good:
  - strong matching between the two panels
  - disciplined grayscale
  - left panel clearly tied to wait time, right panel clearly tied to failed
    access
- creative push:
  - make the pair feel like one designed system, not two separate images
- why it could still fail:
  - mismatched source images would make the slide feel assembled instead of
    designed

### Phone Scheduling Screen

- verdict:
  - `discard`
- why:
  - it pulls the slide toward product-marketing aesthetics and away from system
    proof
  - the phone object becomes too dominant too quickly
- what would make it good:
  - a very documentary, non-glossy scheduling-screen image, which is rare
- creative push:
  - none worth prioritizing versus better options
- why it could still fail:
  - even the best version would likely feel like the wrong deck language
